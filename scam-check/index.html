The scams that look most real

Scam messages work because they copy things that really happen. The ones people fall for most, in the UK, the US, Canada, Australia and Pakistan alike:

  • The parcel fee. "Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay the £1.99 redelivery fee within 24 hours." Couriers don't collect fees by text, and the link goes to a copy of their site.
  • "I sent you money by mistake." A stranger says they paid you by accident, often with a sad reason, and asks you to send it back today. The money that arrived is fake or will be reversed; what you send is real.
  • The bank or account alert. "Unusual activity", "your account is locked", "confirm your details", with a link or a number to call. Banks never ask for codes, PINs or passwords.
  • "Hi Mum, new number." A message pretending to be a child or relative on a new phone, followed by an urgent request for money.
  • The job, the prize, the refund. A part-time job that needs a deposit, a prize you didn't enter, a tax refund that needs your card details.

Three rules that beat almost every scam

  • Never pay, and never share a code, from inside a message. A real company can wait while you check.
  • Check with the company through a route you already trust: the number on your card, the app you already have, the website you type in yourself. Not the link or number in the message.
  • Pressure is the tell. "Within 24 hours", "today", "or your account closes": urgency is there so you don't stop to think.

If you've already tapped a link or sent money: call your bank now using the number on your card, change the password of any account you typed into, and report the message. Acting in the first hour matters.

How Wazirly checks a message for you

Wazirly is a button that floats over every app on your Android phone. Open the message, in Messages, WhatsApp, Gmail, Facebook or anywhere else, tap the button and choose Scam check. It reads what's on the screen and answers in plain words:

  • How risky it looks: strong warning signs, some, or none it can see.
  • The exact phrases that gave it away, like "sent by mistake" or "your account is locked".
  • One clear next step: don't pay, check with your bank, block, report.

It reads the screen only when you tap, and it's a second opinion, not a guarantee: when it isn't sure, it says so, and the three rules above still apply. It works where spam filters stop, inside the chat or inbox you're already looking at, and it answers in English, Urdu or Roman Urdu.

Questions

Can an app tell if a text message is a scam?

It can tell you the warning signs it sees and what to do next, which is what most people need in the moment. No app can be certain about every message, so treat it as a second opinion: if it says strong warning signs, don't pay or share codes, and check with the company through a route you already trust.

Does Wazirly's scam check work on WhatsApp and email?

Yes. It works over any app where the message is on your screen: Messages, WhatsApp, Telegram, Gmail, Outlook, Facebook, Instagram and others. Android hides it only inside the phone's own Settings and on some banking and password screens.

I already replied. What now?

Replying on its own rarely costs anything; paying or sharing a code does. If you sent money, call your bank now using the number on your card. If you typed a password anywhere, change it. Then block the number and report the message to your carrier or the service it came through.
