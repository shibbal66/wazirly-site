Wazirly vs ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot

An on-screen assistant against chat assistants: what each is for, honestly.

Chat assistants are brilliant at conversation: you open their app, type or paste, and talk. Wazirly is a different kind of AI assistant. It's a button that floats over every app on your Android phone; tap it and it reads what's on your screen, then helps with that: the suspicious text in front of you, the chat you can't answer, the setting you can't find. No copying, no switching apps, no describing.

Both have their place. Here's where each one is better.

When to use which

  • Use Wazirly when the problem is on your screen right now: "is this message a scam?", "what do I reply?", "where is the button?", "what does this bill say?", "what does this say in English?"
  • Use a chat assistant when you want to think something through, write something long, or research a topic.
  • Use the phone's own assistant for timers, calls, navigation and hands-free questions.

Questions

Is Wazirly a replacement for ChatGPT or Gemini?

No. They're good at long conversations and writing from scratch. Wazirly is for the moment you're already in an app and stuck: it reads the screen when you tap its button and answers about that. Many people keep both.

Why not just paste the message into a chat assistant?

You can. Wazirly saves the copying, the switching and the explaining, which is exactly where older relatives give up. One tap, and the answer is about the screen they're looking at, in words they can act on.

Does Wazirly use the same AI models?

Wazirly sends the text on your screen, only when you tap, to its own server and an AI model, and shapes the answer for the task: a scam verdict with reasons, three replies in your tone, or a step with a ring around the next button. What it reads isn't sold, used for ads, or used to train AI.

Which should I put on my parents' phone?

For a parent who gets stuck inside apps, the on-screen one: there's nothing to copy or describe, and the scam check is one tap away in the message itself. The privacy policy says exactly what Wazirly reads and keeps.
