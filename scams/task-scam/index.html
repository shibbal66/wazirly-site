Task scams: the job that makes you pay to get paid

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: if a job you didn't apply for pays you to rate apps, like videos or "boost" products, and then asks you to deposit money, it's a scam. The US Federal Trade Commission had about 20,000 reports of these task scams in the first half of 2024, against about 5,000 in all of 2023 (FTC). In Canada, job scams cost people CAD 50.6 million in 2025 (Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre).

What the message looks like

Example of a first message. Written for this page.

Hi, I'm Emma, a recruiter. We have a flexible remote job, 60 to 90 minutes a day, paid daily. Are you interested?

Example of a task. Written for this page.

Today's task: complete 40 product ratings to boost app rankings. Your commission is paid after each set.

Example of the ask. Written for this page.

To unlock the next set and withdraw your commission, top up your account with $300 in crypto.

How it unfolds, step by step

  1. The opener. An unexpected message on WhatsApp, Telegram, text or social media about making quick money online (FTC).
  2. Building trust. A friendly "mentor" trains you. The tasks have names like optimization or product boosting, and an app or website shows your earnings growing.
  3. Moving you to another app. A group chat full of other workers sharing their success. They're part of it.
  4. The bait. A small real payment, usually $5 to $20 (FTC).
  5. The ask. To unlock the next set of tasks, often sets of 40, or to withdraw what you've earned, you have to deposit your own money, usually crypto (FTC).
  6. The pressure. Each deposit unlocks a bigger one. The group cheers you on.
  7. The exit. The earnings never come out. Even the deposit doesn't release them.

The signs on your screen

  • A job offer you didn't apply for, by text or a messaging app.
  • Vague work with words like optimization, product boosting, commission, levels.
  • Paying in before you can be paid out.
  • Crypto as the only way to pay.

What to do now

Never pay to get paid. The FTC calls it a sure sign of a scam (FTC). Stop the tasks, leave the group and stop replying.

If you already paid

Contact your bank and any crypto exchange you used, now. Keep the chats, the app or site's name, and the wallet addresses you paid. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online.
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101. If you sent a bank transfer, your bank must usually pay you back, up to £85,000, if you report it within 13 months; a £100 excess may apply (Payment Systems Regulator).
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Is a task job real if they paid me once?

No. The small first payment is part of the scam: it builds trust before they ask you to deposit your own money. The FTC says these payments are usually $5 to $20.

Do real jobs ask you to deposit money?

No. A real employer pays you; it doesn't ask you to pay to unlock work or withdraw your earnings. If a job asks for a deposit, stop.

I deposited money to unlock my earnings. What now?

Stop depositing: more money won't release the earnings. Contact your bank and the crypto exchange, keep your records, and report it to the fraud service for your country.
