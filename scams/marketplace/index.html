Marketplace scams: fake buyers and fake sellers

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: keep the chat and the payment on the platform, never share a code, and see an item before you pay a stranger by bank transfer. This guide covers buying and selling on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, Gumtree, Kijiji and similar sites. Purchase scams, where you pay for something that never comes, were 71% of the push-payment fraud cases UK banks recorded in 2025 (UK Finance).

What the message looks like

Example message to a seller. Written for this page.

Is this still available? I'll take it, no need to see it. Before I pay I want to check you're real, so I'm sending you a code. Can you tell me what it says?

Example message to a seller. Written for this page.

I've paid you. The payment is on hold until you click this link to confirm and receive it: [link]

Example message to a buyer. Written for this page.

It's yours if you send a deposit by bank transfer today. Lots of people are asking.

How it unfolds, step by step

If you're selling:

  1. The opener. Is this still available? Then a quick yes, with no questions about the item.
  2. Moving you to another app. They ask for your number or email, to chat away from the platform.
  3. The bait. I've paid, or a courier will collect.
  4. The ask. A code, to check you're real, which lets them set up a Google Voice number linked to your phone number (FTC); a link to receive or confirm the payment; a fee for the courier; or a check for more than your price and a request to send back the difference (FTC).
  5. The pressure. They're in a hurry, and the courier is on the way.
  6. The exit. The payment was fake and the item is gone, or a check bounces weeks after you sent the difference.

If you're buying:

  1. The opener. An item well below its usual price.
  2. Moving you to another app. The seller wants to chat and be paid away from the platform.
  3. The ask. A deposit or the full price by bank transfer, before you see anything.
  4. The pressure. Lots of people are interested, so pay today.
  5. The exit. No item, and the seller disappears.

The signs on your screen

  • A buyer who wants a code from you, for any reason.
  • A payment that's on hold until you click a link or pay a fee.
  • A check for more than your price.
  • A seller who won't let you see the item, and wants a bank transfer now.
  • Any move off the platform to chat or pay.

What to do now

Never share a code, and don't send anything until the money is really in your account. The FTC's advice to sellers: don't accept a mobile payment from someone you don't know, and never deposit a check for more than the selling price (FTC). If you're buying, see the item first, or pay with a method that protects you.

If you already paid

Call your bank or card company now, on the number on your card. If you paid with a payment app, report it in the app and ask for the payment to be reversed (FTC). Report the account to the marketplace too. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online. If you shared a Google Voice code, you can take your number back at voice.google.com/regain (FTC).
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101. If you sent a bank transfer, your bank must usually pay you back, up to £85,000, if you report it within 13 months; a £100 excess may apply (Payment Systems Regulator).
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Why does a buyer want me to send them a code?

Because the code lets them take over something of yours, often a Google Voice number linked to your phone number. The FTC's advice: never share a verification code with someone you don't know.

The buyer says they paid but the money is on hold. Is that real?

Almost certainly not. The FTC warns that scammers send fake payment notices and hope you send the item before you notice. Check your bank or payment app yourself, and don't click their link.

Should I pay a deposit by bank transfer?

Not to a stranger before you've seen the item. A bank transfer is hard to get back. In the UK, banks usually reimburse transfers lost to scams up to £85,000 if you report within 13 months, but it's far better not to need it.

I gave a buyer my Google Voice code. What now?

Go to voice.google.com/regain and enter your number to take it back, as the FTC suggests. Then report the buyer to the marketplace and at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
