Tax office or government call or text: is it a scam?

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: if a text has a link to claim a tax refund, or a caller threatens arrest unless you pay today, it's a scam. Every agency below says it doesn't work that way. In the US, people reported losing about $920 million to government impersonators in 2025 (FTC).

What the message looks like

Example of a scam call script, US. Written for this page.

This is the Social Security Administration. Your Social Security number has been suspended because of suspicious activity. Stay on the line to avoid arrest.

Example of a scam text, UK. Written for this page.

HMRC: You are due a tax refund of £326.80. Claim it here: [link]

Example of a scam text, Australia. Written for this page.

myGov: Your details need updating to keep receiving payments. Sign in: [link]

How it unfolds, step by step

  1. The opener. A text, email or recorded call using an official name, with either good news (a refund) or bad news (a debt, a problem with your number).
  2. Building trust. Official words: a case number, a badge number, a department name, maybe your real details.
  3. The bait. A refund you can claim, or a crisis: your identity is linked to a crime, which the FTC lists as one of the three stories behind the biggest losses (FTC).
  4. The ask. Sign in through the link, or pay now by gift card, crypto, wire transfer, e-transfer or cash.
  5. The pressure. Arrest, deportation, a fine, a frozen account. Stay on the line, and tell no one.
  6. The exit. More fees, or a hand-off to another caller, from a bank or another agency, who tells you to move money to protect it (FBI).

The signs on your screen

A link to claim money or sign in, a threat of arrest, or a demand to pay today by gift card, crypto or transfer. And what each agency says about itself:

  • IRS (US): its first contact is normally a letter in the post, and it doesn't start contact by email, text or social media to ask for personal or financial information (IRS).
  • Social Security (US): it will never threaten you with arrest, suspend your Social Security number, ask you to pay with gift cards, crypto or cash, or offer to move your money to a protected account (SSA). Report impostors at oig.ssa.gov/report.
  • HMRC (UK): it will never tell you about a tax rebate or ask for personal or payment details by text. Forward suspicious texts to 60599 (charged at your network rate) and emails to phishing@hmrc.gov.uk (GOV.UK).
  • CRA (Canada): it will never send refunds by e-transfer or text, demand payment by e-transfer, crypto, prepaid cards or gift cards, or threaten to arrest or deport you (CRA).
  • ATO (Australia): it will never send an unsolicited text with a link. To check a contact, call the ATO on 1800 008 540, and send scam screenshots to ReportScams@ato.gov.au (ATO).
  • myGov (Australia): Services Australia won't send you a link in a text or email; a myGov text with a link is a scam (Services Australia).

What to do now

Hang up, or close the text without tapping the link. Then contact the agency yourself, through its official website typed into your browser or a number from that site, and ask if anything is really wrong.

If you already paid

Call your bank now, on the number on your card, and ask them to stop the payment. If you paid by gift card, call the card's company and tell them a scammer has the card number and PIN. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online. If you gave your Social Security number, IdentityTheft.gov makes a recovery plan.
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101. If you sent a bank transfer, your bank must usually pay you back, up to £85,000, if you report it within 13 months; a £100 excess may apply (Payment Systems Regulator).
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Does the IRS send text messages?

The IRS says its first contact is normally a letter in the post, and that it doesn't start contact by text, email or social media to ask for personal or financial information. A text from the IRS with a link is a scam sign.

Is the HMRC tax refund text real?

No. HMRC says it never tells people about a tax rebate by text. Forward the text to 60599 and delete it.

Can the CRA arrest me if I don't pay today?

No. The CRA says it will never threaten to arrest or deport you, and never demands payment by gift card, crypto or e-transfer. Hang up and report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

Does myGov send links by text?

No. Services Australia says it won't send you a link in a text or email, so a myGov text with a link is a scam. Sign in by typing my.gov.au yourself or using the myGov app.
