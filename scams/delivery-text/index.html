Parcel delivery fee text: is it a scam?

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: almost always, yes. Fake delivery texts were the most-reported text scam in the US in 2024, usually pretending to be the US Postal Service (FTC). In a study of 1.3 million scam texts reported to 7726 in the UK, parcel and delivery lures were one of the most common kinds (Agarwal and others, 2025). Don't use the link: check with the courier in its own app or website, with the tracking number the shop gave you.

What the message looks like

Example of a scam text, US. Written for this page.

USPS: Your package could not be delivered due to an incomplete address. Update it within 24 hours or it will be returned: [link]

Example of a scam text, UK. Written for this page.

Your parcel is being held due to a £1.99 unpaid shipping fee. Pay now to arrange delivery: [link]

Example of a scam text, Canada. Written for this page.

Delivery attempt failed. Confirm your details and pay a $1.10 redelivery fee to release your item: [link]

How it unfolds, step by step

  1. The opener. A text about a parcel, from a number you don't know. These texts go out in bulk, so some land on a day you really are expecting a parcel. That's luck, not a sign it's real.
  2. Building trust. It borrows a courier's name and the look of its website.
  3. The pressure. Within 24 hours, or the parcel goes back.
  4. The bait. A tiny fee, or just a request to update your address. Small asks feel harmless.
  5. The ask. The page asks for your card details and personal details, sometimes your Social Security number (FTC).
  6. The second ask. Some pages then ask for the one-time code your bank sends. The same kind of kit is used for toll and postal texts: the code adds your card to a phone wallet on the criminal's phone (KrebsOnSecurity).
  7. The exit. No parcel, and the card is used later.

The signs on your screen

  • A tracking text with a link from a courier you didn't sign up with. USPS says it only texts people who asked for updates with a tracking number, and its texts don't contain links (US Postal Inspection Service).
  • A fee to release a parcel. Canada Post says it will never text you to ask for card or banking details or for payment for a delivery (Canada Post); Australia Post says it will never text you to ask for payment (Australia Post).
  • A web address that isn't the courier's own.
  • A request for a code your bank sent you.

What to do now

Don't use the link. Open the courier's own app, or type its website yourself, and look up the tracking number from your order confirmation.

Then report it and delete it. In the US, the UK and Canada, forward the text to 7726, the number phone companies use to spot and block scam senders (NCSC; Canada Post). In the US you can also send USPS lookalikes to spam@uspis.gov. In Australia, report it to Scamwatch.

If you already paid

Call your bank or card company now, on the number on the back of your card, and ask them to cancel the card. If you typed in a code from your bank, tell them that too. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online. If you gave your Social Security number, IdentityTheft.gov makes a recovery plan.
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101.
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Is the missed delivery text real?

Usually not. The real way to check is the courier's own app or website with the tracking number the shop gave you, never the link in the text. Fake delivery texts were the most-reported text scam in the US in 2024.

Do couriers ever text about a fee?

Some couriers send real tracking texts. But USPS says its texts come only if you asked for them and contain no links, Canada Post says it never texts asking for payment, and Australia Post says it never texts or emails to ask for payment. When in doubt, check in the courier's own app.

I entered my card details. What now?

Call your bank on the number on the back of your card and ask them to cancel it. Tell them if you typed in a code they sent. Then report the text: 7726 in the US, the UK and Canada, Scamwatch in Australia.

Why did it come on the day I was expecting a parcel?

Because these texts are sent in bulk, and on any day lots of people are expecting a parcel. The timing is luck, not a sign the text is real.
