"Hi Mum, new number": the scam, step by step

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: if a message from a new number says it's your child and soon asks for money, treat it as a scam until you've called your child on the number you already have. Which? reported in 2022 that the scam had spread from WhatsApp to ordinary texts (Which?). It also arrives as Hi Mom, Hi Dad, or a call in a voice that sounds like a relative: the FBI counted more than $5 million lost in 2025 to such "distress" calls using voice cloning (FBI IC3 2025 report).

What the message looks like

Example of a scam text, based on one quoted by Which?

Hello Mum, I've broken my phone and I'm using a friend's old one. I need to talk, it's urgent, can you text me on WhatsApp on my new number please

Example of a scam message. Written for this page.

Hi Dad, it's me, this is my new number. Save it and delete the old one x

Example of the follow-up. Written for this page.

Mum, I need a favour. I've got a bill due today and I can't get into my banking on this phone. Can you pay it for me? I'll pay you back tomorrow. Here are the details:

How it unfolds, step by step

  1. The opener. A message from a number you don't know: Hi Mum, Hi Mom, Hi Dad. There's often no name, so it fits anyone with children.
  2. Building trust. A short, warm chat. They ask you to save the new number and delete the old one.
  3. Moving you to another app. If it starts as a text, it asks you to carry on in WhatsApp.
  4. The bait. A problem they can't fix on the new phone: a bill due today, a banking app that won't work.
  5. The ask. Pay this for me, I'll pay you back. The bank details usually aren't in your child's name (Which?).
  6. The pressure. It's urgent, it has to be today, and they may have a reason why they can't talk on the phone right now.
  7. The exit. If you pay, a second request may follow.

The signs on your screen

  • A new number that says "Mum", "Mom" or "Dad" instead of using your name.
  • A request to delete the old number, or to move to WhatsApp.
  • Money for someone else's account, today.
  • A reason why they can't call or send a voice note.

What to do now

Call your child on the number you already have. If they don't answer, ask the new number something only your child would know, or ask for a voice note (Which?). Don't send money until you've spoken to them.

Then report it: if it came as a text, forward it to 7726 in the UK, the US and Canada. In Australia, report it to Scamwatch.

If you already paid

Call your bank now, on the number on the back of your card. Ask them to stop or recall the payment. The sooner you call, the better the chance. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online.
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101. If you sent a bank transfer, your bank must usually pay you back, up to £85,000, if you report it within 13 months; a £100 excess may apply (Payment Systems Regulator).
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Is the Hi Mum new number text a scam?

Very often, yes. A message from a new number that says it's your child and then asks for money is a well-known scam. Call your child on the number you already have before you do anything else.

How do I check it's really my child?

Call the old number. If you can't reach them, ask the new number a question only your child could answer, or ask for a voice note. A scammer will find a reason not to.

I already sent money. What now?

Call your bank now on the number on your card and ask them to stop or recall the payment. In the UK, bank transfers lost to scams are usually reimbursed up to £85,000 if you report within 13 months. Then report it to the police service for your country.

Does it happen in the US, Canada and Australia too?

Yes, as Hi Mom, Hi Dad, or a call in a voice that copies a relative. The FBI counted more than $5 million lost in 2025 to distress calls that used voice cloning.
