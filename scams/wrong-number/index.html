The wrong number text that keeps chatting

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: a wrong-number text on its own may be a real mistake. The scam is what follows: a stranger who keeps chatting, moves you to WhatsApp or Telegram, and weeks later talks about investing. This is often called pig butchering. Investment scams like it are the costliest kind: the FBI counted $7.2 billion lost to crypto investment fraud in the US in 2025 (FBI IC3 2025 report). In a UK study of reports to 7726, "wrong number" openers were the most common scam text of all (Agarwal and others, 2025).

What the message looks like

Example of a first text. Written for this page.

Hi, is this Anna? It's Lily from the yoga class, are we still on for Thursday?

Example of the reply after you say wrong number. Written for this page.

Oh I'm so sorry! I must have saved it wrong. You're kind to reply, most people don't. Where are you based?

Example, weeks later. Written for this page.

My uncle showed me a trading platform and I made 30% this month. I can show you how, just start small.

How it unfolds, step by step

  1. The opener. A text for someone else. In interviews with 26 victims, 18 were first contacted this way (Oak and others, 2025).
  2. Building trust. Daily messages for weeks or months, often with romance or friendship. In the same study this stage lasted 3 to 11 months. Photos are common; video calls are rare.
  3. Moving you to another app. From texts or a dating app to WhatsApp or Telegram.
  4. The bait. Talk of their own trading success, screenshots of profits, an uncle or a mentor with inside knowledge.
  5. The ask. Open an account on a platform or app they name, and start small. In the study, first deposits were $20 to $125, and the account then showed fake gains. Some people were even allowed a small withdrawal.
  6. The pressure. Bigger deposits, a deal that ends soon, a loan or a credit card, and guilt if you hesitate.
  7. The exit. When you try to take money out, it's blocked, or you're told to pay a tax or fee first. Then they disappear.
  8. The recovery scam. More than half the victims in the study were then contacted by fake investigators or recovery firms. See recovery scams.

The signs on your screen

  • A stranger who keeps chatting after you say wrong number.
  • A move to WhatsApp or Telegram.
  • Crypto, USDT, a trading platform, signals, a mentor.
  • Screenshots of profits, and an invitation to start small.
  • Requests to keep it between the two of you.
  • A fee or tax before you can withdraw.

What to do now

Don't send money or crypto to any platform that someone you've only met by message recommends. Talk to someone you trust before you send anything: in US research, people who discussed a scam with no one while it was happening were more likely to lose money (FINRA Foundation).

If the chat began with a wrong number, stop replying. You don't owe a stranger an explanation.

If you already paid

Contact your bank and the crypto exchange you used, now. Ask them to stop any payments still pending. Keep the screenshots, the platform's name and address, and the wallet addresses you paid. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online. The FBI says to report at ic3.gov as soon as you can, with full transaction details.
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101. If you sent a bank transfer, your bank must usually pay you back, up to £85,000, if you report it within 13 months; a £100 excess may apply (Payment Systems Regulator).
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Is a wrong number text a scam?

Not always: people do mistype numbers. The warning sign is what happens next. A stranger who keeps chatting after you say wrong number, moves you to WhatsApp or Telegram, and later mentions investing or crypto is following a known scam pattern.

Should I reply to a wrong number text?

You don't have to. If you do, keep it to wrong number and don't carry on the chat. Replying on its own rarely costs anything; sending money or crypto does.

What is pig butchering?

A long scam in which someone builds a friendship or romance by message over weeks or months, then persuades the victim to invest on a fake platform. The platform shows fake gains until the victim tries to withdraw, when fees appear and the scammer disappears.

I invested through someone I met online. What now?

Stop sending money, contact your bank and the exchange, keep every record, and report it: ic3.gov in the US, Report Fraud in the UK, the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, or Scamwatch and ReportCyber in Australia. Ignore anyone who offers to recover it for a fee.
