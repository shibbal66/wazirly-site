The fake bank fraud call and the safe account

Part of the Wazirly scam library. Updated 28 November 2026.

Short answer: your bank will never ask you to move money to keep it safe. UK banks say so in their Take Five campaign (Take Five), and the US Federal Trade Commission says the same: don't move money to protect it (FTC). In the US, people reported losing nearly $1 billion to bank impersonators in 2025 (FTC). Hang up and call your bank on the number on your card.

What the message looks like

Example of the first text. Written for this page.

Bank alert: Did you attempt a payment of £1,250.00 to J. Smith? Reply YES or NO.

Example of what the caller says. Written for this page.

This is the fraud team. Your account has been compromised and we think someone at your branch is involved. To protect your money we'll move it to a new safe account. Please don't discuss this with branch staff.

Example, Canada. Written for this page.

This is your bank's investigator. To confirm your identity, please read me the code we just sent to your phone.

How it unfolds, step by step

  1. The opener. A text about a payment you didn't make, asking you to reply YES or NO or to call (FTC), or a call that shows your bank's name. Caller ID can be faked (Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre).
  2. Building trust. They may already know your name and some of your details. They sound calm and professional, and pass you to an investigator or a manager.
  3. The bait. Fear: someone is stealing your money right now.
  4. The ask. Move your money to a safe account, read out a code from a text, hand your card to a courier, or install an app so they can help (Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre).
  5. The pressure. Stay on the line, don't tell the branch, act now. Some callers even talk people through their bank's own warnings: "OK, this is going to come up now, you need to press yes" (Which?).
  6. The exit. The safe account belongs to the scammer. Sometimes a second caller, from the police or a government agency, follows to make it feel official (FBI).

The signs on your screen

  • Anyone asking you to move money to protect it.
  • A request to read out a code, a PIN or a password.
  • A courier coming to collect your card.
  • Being told to keep it from your bank's staff, or to ignore your bank's warnings.
  • A request to install an app while they're on the phone.

What to do now

Hang up, and call your bank on the number on the back of your card. In the UK you can call 159, which connects you to most banks (Stop Scams UK). The FTC's advice is the same everywhere: hang up and check with a number you know is real (FTC).

If you already paid

Call your bank now, on the number on your card, and say you've been the victim of a scam. Ask them to stop or recall the payment. Then report it where you live:

  • US: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and at ic3.gov if it happened online. Speed matters: in 2025 the FBI's Recovery Asset Team froze $146.6 million, with a 56% success rate, when people reported quickly (FBI IC3 2025 report).
  • UK: call 159 to reach your own bank (Stop Scams UK). Report it to Report Fraud, online or on 0300 123 2040, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (it replaced Action Fraud in December 2025), or to Police Scotland on 101. If you sent a bank transfer, your bank must usually pay you back, up to £85,000, if you report it within 13 months; a £100 excess may apply (Payment Systems Regulator).
  • Canada: report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca or 1-888-495-8501, and to your local police.
  • Australia: report it to Scamwatch, and to ReportCyber if you lost money online. IDCARE helps for free on 1800 595 160 if you shared personal details.

Every number and website, country by country, is on Report a scam.

Questions

Would my bank ever ask me to move money to a safe account?

No. Banks and the police never ask you to move money to keep it safe. Anyone who does is a scammer, even if the call shows your bank's name.

The call showed my bank's number. Is it real?

Not necessarily. Caller ID can be faked to show a bank's real number. Hang up and call the number on the back of your card yourself.

I moved money. Can I get it back?

Call your bank now. In the UK, bank transfers lost to scams are usually reimbursed up to £85,000 if you report within 13 months. In other countries it depends on how you paid, and speed matters.

What is 159?

A UK phone number run by Stop Scams UK that connects you to your own bank. If a call or text about your account worries you, hang up and call 159.
